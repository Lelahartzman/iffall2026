THE POWER OF NUMBERS

Evidence
for Change.

Florence Nightingale believed that numbers could reveal problems that might otherwise be ignored.

During and after the Crimean War, she gathered and analyzed information about illness, mortality, and hospital conditions.

Her statistical work demonstrated the devastating impact of preventable disease and helped strengthen the case for sanitary reform.

RECOGNITION

1858

Nightingale becomes the first woman elected a Fellow of the Royal Statistical Society.

VISUALIZING INFORMATION

A Picture Worth
a Thousand Numbers.

Nightingale used polar area diagrams to communicate complex information about causes of death among soldiers during the Crimean War.

Why It Mattered

The diagrams made patterns of mortality easier to understand and helped communicate the importance of sanitary reform to decision-makers.

Designing with Data

Nightingale recognized that visual information could be more persuasive and accessible than tables of numbers alone.

A LASTING LEGACY

Her Influence
Lives On.

Nightingale continued advocating for public health, hospital reform, and nursing education throughout her life. Her work influenced healthcare in Britain and abroad.

Today, she is remembered not only as a pioneer of modern nursing, but also as an important figure in the history of statistics and evidence-based reform.
