1854 / SCUTARI

Answering
the Call.

In 1854, during the Crimean War, Florence Nightingale led a group of 38 nurses to care for wounded British soldiers.

They arrived at the military hospital in Scutari, near Constantinople (modern-day Istanbul).

What they found was devastating. The hospital was overcrowded, poorly ventilated, and lacked adequate sanitation and supplies.

HOSPITAL CONDITIONS

A Crisis Beyond
the Battlefield.

Disease posed an enormous threat to hospitalized soldiers. Infections such as cholera and typhus spread through overcrowded and unsanitary conditions.

Nightingale organized nursing care, improved the distribution of supplies, and advocated for better hygiene. A government sanitary commission also made major improvements to drainage and ventilation in 1855.

THE LADY WITH THE LAMP

A Symbol
of Compassion.

Nightingale became famous for checking on wounded soldiers during the night.

Reports of her walking through hospital wards carrying a lamp inspired the nickname "The Lady with the Lamp."

Her nighttime rounds became a lasting symbol of care, dedication, and humanity.
