A NEW APPROACH

Changing
the System.

After returning from the Crimean War in 1856, Florence Nightingale focused on reforming healthcare in Britain and beyond.

She believed that hospitals needed better sanitation, ventilation, organization, and trained staff.

Her recommendations helped change how hospitals were designed and how patients were cared for.

A LANDMARK PUBLICATION

1859

Nightingale publishes Notes on Nursing, a foundational text on patient care.

PRINCIPLES OF CARE

Health Begins

Nightingale emphasized that the environment surrounding a patient was an essential part of recovery.

✦

Cleanliness

Clean rooms, bedding, and surroundings helped reduce the risk of illness.

✦

Fresh Air

Proper ventilation was essential to creating healthier hospital environments.

✦

Nutrition

PROFESSIONAL EDUCATION

A School
for Change.

In 1860, the Nightingale Training School for Nurses opened at St Thomas' Hospital in London.

The school provided structured training and practical experience for aspiring nurses.

Its graduates went on to work in hospitals and establish nursing programs elsewhere, spreading Nightingale's approach to professional care.

A NEW ERA

1860

The Nightingale Training School opens at St Thomas' Hospital in London.

A LASTING CHANGE

✦
