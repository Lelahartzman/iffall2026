CHILDHOOD

Born into
Privilege.

Florence Nightingale was born on May 12, 1820, in Florence, Italy, to a wealthy British family. She was named after the city of her birth.

Her father, William Edward Nightingale, believed strongly in education and personally taught Florence and her older sister, Parthenope.

Florence studied mathematics, languages, history, and philosophy. Her education helped develop the analytical skills that would later become essential to her work.

CHALLENGING EXPECTATIONS

A Different Path.

During the Victorian era, women from wealthy families were generally expected to marry and manage their households. Professional nursing was not considered a respectable career for a woman of Florence's standing.

But Florence felt drawn toward helping others. In 1837, she recorded experiencing what she understood as a calling from God. She eventually came to believe that nursing was the work she was meant to do.

EDUCATION

Learning to
Make a Difference.

Despite her family's initial objections, Florence pursued practical training in nursing.

In 1851, she trained at the Institution of Protestant Deaconesses in Kaiserswerth, Germany.

Two years later, she became superintendent of the Institute for the Care of Sick Gentlewomen in Upper Harley Street, London.

Her experience in hospital administration and patient care prepared her for the challenges that lay ahead.
